What is a Makhie?

Start with the neighbour.

In isiZulu and isiXhosa, your neighbour is uMakhelwane — makhi, when you greet them over the fence. And everyone who grew up in a township or a village knows exactly what a makhi does.

A makhi arrives without being asked. Sugar when yours runs out. A ladder over the wall. A lift to town when the taxi money is short. Eyes on your children when you work late. A hand at the funeral, a chair and a plate at the wedding. Nobody writes an invoice for any of it — because a good neighbour knows that your standing and theirs are the same standing.

That is ubuntu: umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu — a person is a person through other people. I am because we are. Not charity from above; a hand from beside you.

Why Makhie exists.

The corner shop deserves the same paperwork as the corporation. But most small businesses here started with a phone, a hustle and no financial tools at all — no invoicing system, no booking desk, no accounting package, no one to make the bank believe the R30 000 a month that is really there.

Makhie exists to close that gap the way a neighbour would: free, online, and without conditions. Financial and educational tools for the business owners who were never handed any — so that the underprivileged creator can compete with, and look like, the businesses that have everything. Looking established shouldn't be a privilege. It should be a download away.

So that's what this is.

Makhie is the digital makhi for your hustle. It lends you what the big companies pay for — the receipts and agreements, the calculators, the booking calendar, the evidence pack for the bank — and like a real neighbour, it doesn't send a bill for the help.

Because when the street's businesses stand, the street stands. Ubuntu, in software.
